# Chapter 5: Closing the Design-Test-Learn Loop

An agent can support an iterative experimental workflow: 
1. Propose what to test.
2. Interpret the resulting evidence.
3. Revise the next decision.

We will:
- Turn a research objective into testable hypotheses and measurable predictions.
- Assess whether a proposed experiment can distinguish between competing explanations.
- Identify controls, constraints, and decisions that require a scientist's judgement.
- Compare experimental results with the original predictions.
- Use those results to prioritize the next experiment and explain why the plan changed.

**Research objective:** find a protease that cleaves a specified target peptide while avoiding a panel of similar off-target peptides

## 5.1 Design

- Define the research objective
- Propose hypotheses and experiments
- Review before testing

Give an Aviary agent the research objective and a literature search tool, it searches for proteases and reported recognition squences, and extracts an evidence table.

The agent proposes a candidate panel. For each candidate, it states a predicted result for the target and off-target peptodes and explains its rationale.

The researcher reviews the proposal against the evidence and decides whether the predictions are supported, then approves or revises the test panel before running the experiment.

## 5.2 Test

- Define the experiment
- Researcher review
- Run the experiment

The agent receives the approved candidate proteases and assay constraints. It proposes the target and off-target peptides to measure for each protease, plate layout with replicates and controls, measurements to record over time, plan for background correction and comparing target with off-target cleavage, etc.

A separate scenario generator receives the approved test panel and creates synthetic results. The researcher inspects the results before passing them to the agent.

## 5.3 Learn

- Analyze the results
- Update the next decision
- Plan another round

The agent receives the results, its original predictions, and the researcher's approved test plan. The researcher asks the agent to identify which predictions help up, which failed, and what remains uncertain.

The agent updates its candidate ranking, proposes the next experiment with a reason for the change, and explains what the proposed experiment would distinguish. The researcher reviews the interpretation and decides whether to approve the next round.
