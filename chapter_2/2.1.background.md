# 2.1 AI in Scientific Research

<span class="update-edit">Biological research produces enormous amounts of information in many forms: sequences, images, structures, publications, etc. Each type of data offeres a different view of a biological question, but the volume and variety make it difficult to organize, compare, and connect findings across sources. Scientists can use AI to find patterns in different kinds of data. Each model is built for a particular kind of input and question. For example, [AlphaFold](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03819-2) predicts protein structure from amino acid sequences, while language models take papers and questions as input and generate text in the form of answers or summaries. In this chapter, we focus on language models and how the information supplied to them shapes their answers.</span>

<span class="update-edit">Without supplied sources, a language model generates an answer based on its training. A relevant paper or abstract provides evidence from a specific study that can be checked against the source. Retrieving multiple studies allows the model to compare findings across a broader body of literature. Other questions call for different sources. By retrieving relevant records before asking the model to respond, the same approach can be applied to information beyond the scientific literature. For example, [UniProt](https://www.uniprot.org/) provides protein sequences and annotations that can help investigate a kinase’s function, while the [Protein Data Bank](https://www.rcsb.org/) provides structural records that can help examine its three-dimensional form.</span>

The goal is to provide scientists with hands-on `python` examples that demonstrate how modern AI tools can be integrated into everyday research workflows. The examples that follow illustrate how large language models and related AI systems can accelerate tasks such as analyzing datasets, extracting information from scientific literature, and building intelligent research assistants.

These examples will teach you how to build AI systems that:

- Query and summarize scientific literature

- Build retrieval systems that search large collections of papers or data

- Extract structured information from online, open-source datasets

The accompanying `python notebooks` provide step-by-step implementations that readers can run and modify for their own research projects. These notebooks demonstrate techniques such as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for literature search and LLM model–based dataset parsing for biological data analysis.
